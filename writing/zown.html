zown

applied ai intern · april to september 2026

they say you can never find the same person twice (even in the same person!). that's how i felt after my stint at zown, and how i feel writing this now, heading back to school. i spent the last 7 months at zown as the 5th engineer, working on zoro (the ai realtor), an api that serves home data, and hubspot: real-time property search and filtering, photo delivery, property history and analytics, valuations, alerts, webhooks, so anything we built had clean data under it.

preface: what is zown

zown is a digital real estate platform in canada aimed at making home buying more accessible by returning part of the commission directly to the buyers.

how it started

eight months ago i was at an intimate iftaar event. i had the option to leave toronto or stay, and being the oldest from a brown household, my parents were adamant that i spend my first summer of university in toronto (no cali just bust). i met rishard rameez, he asked me what i was up to this summer, i told him i was looking for something, and he asked me, "can you come in tomorrow morning?"

and that's how it began.

first impressions

first day i came in with the feeling that i might be starting behind, not knowing how the startup operates, not knowing how anything functions. but from day one i was given full autonomy to pick what i wanted to work on, so i started looking for the biggest bottleneck i could find.

my experience started with applied ai, and that is what i was hired for, but before i could ever begin working on an autonomous ai realtor, i had to start by figuring out the data situation.

zoro

my role was made clear: drive forward the automation for realtors, mainly response times, cma (comparative market analysis) generations, and real estate knowledge. i met asanka pradeep, who had been working on an mvp sales ai tool that listened and answered questions on whatsapp.

when talking with rishard, we came to the conclusion that it was important to suit the agent not only according to information, but keep it specific to the stage of the customer. that's when zoro, the ai realtor, really began.

i spent the majority of my time building zoro, evolving an internal sales tool developed to assist our account managers with closing homes. we started with a mock version in python using mastra, and after 11 chats we switched over to deploying scalable infra on aws.

the api

underneath everything sat the data work: an api that serves home data, real-time property search and filtering, photo delivery, property history and analytics, valuations, alerts, webhooks. it was the plumbing that let zoro actually know what it was talking about.

hubspot

i think the first time i got access to hubspot at zown i almost shed a tear, and not because it was beautiful.

in order for our agent to reference customer stages, the source of data had to be clean enough to actually understand the customer. the challenge: each object was attached to 700+ properties and tangled in multiple workflows. at the time, i was recommended to go through it by hand.

dr. awad & i took a bomboclaude of agents and subjugated the processes and properties one by one. the most enjoyable part wasn't unweeding the crm, it was introducing omar to claude, claude code, and watching him in real time fall in love with using mcps. back in april, hubspot was just launching their mcp, so we were using a special app to go through and manage it.

well wishes

i'm grateful to rishard for the "come in tomorrow morning", to asanka for the foundation to build on, and to the team for trusting an intern with real customers. the bar at zown was never "does the demo look good." it was "would you let it talk to your customer unsupervised at 2am." the answer had to be yes.
