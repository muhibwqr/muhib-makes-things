what abdurrahman ibn awf knew about money

lessons from the richest sahabi · 6 min

most of us get our money advice from the wrong people. dave ramsey, robert kiyosaki, a tiktoker with a ring light. that is a shame, because we have our own teachers, and one of them ran circles around all of them fourteen hundred years ago.

abdurrahman ibn awf, may allah be pleased with him, was one of the ten companions promised paradise, and one of the wealthiest men of his time. he once rolled into madinah with a caravan of seven hundred camels, each one worth something like a supercar today, and gave the whole thing away in an afternoon. the generosity is the easy part to admire. the strategy underneath it is what we forget. here is what he actually did.

he asked for the market, not a handout

when he migrated to madinah he had nothing. the prophet, peace be upon him, paired him with a man who offered him half his wealth, even to divorce a wife so abdurrahman could marry. he refused all of it. "just show me the way to the market." he started trading dried yoghurt and butter, from zero, by choice. allah does not change a people until they change what is in themselves. self-reliance is not pride here. it is the default our deen sets for us.

he sold honestly, and it compounded

he inspected his own goods. if something was flawed he marked it flawed and dropped the price instead of hiding it. that is ihsan, excellence when no one is checking, and it built a reputation that brought customers back. trust is the cheapest marketing there is and the most expensive thing to fake.

he funded his own competition

this one is hard for me. he gave interest-free loans to small traders who could have grown into rivals. he did not hoard capital to stay on top. he lent it out clean, no riba, and let other people rise. and they did, and many brought their business back to him out of loyalty. cooperate in righteousness, the quran says. he read that as a strategy, not just a sentiment.

he carried a city's debts

talha said one-third of madinah owed debts that abdurrahman paid off, one-third took his interest-free loans, and one-third lived on his charity outright. all of madinah, he said, lives off abdurrahman. imagine your wealth being load-bearing for a whole city.

he gave the caravan away

when he heard the prophet, peace be upon him, warn about the test of wealth in the next life, he wept and donated the seven hundred camels on the spot. charity does not decrease wealth, the prophet said, and abdurrahman lived the proof. it kept coming back, multiplied. the money chased him precisely because he refused to be owned by it.

he feared his own comfort

a lavish meal was once set in front of him and he wept, remembering a companion who died with almost nothing. "i fear we have been given our reward in this life instead of the next." a man already guaranteed paradise, still auditing his own heart.

he kept the books on his soul

he literally tallied it. how many camels, how much gold, against how much he had given in the path of allah. let every soul look to what it has sent ahead for tomorrow. he made gratitude an action, not a feeling, and then he checked the balance.

none of it was a trade-off

that is the part that gets me. he did not get rich despite his deen, or stay poor because of it. the wealth and the worship were the same motion. you can build something large and keep your heart in the next life. you just have to hold it open-handed.

so before i take money advice from a stranger online, i want to sit with men like this first. that is the wealth i actually want: load-bearing for the people around me, and weightless on my own heart. inshallah.

lessons drawn from the life of abdurrahman ibn awf (RA), through tabaqat ibn sa'd and the hadith collections. i first read a fuller telling on islamicfinanceguru.
