Backpropagation, Ambition, and Injustice

originally posted on linkedin · august 2, 2026 · 5 min

Assalamu-alaykum (peace be upon you). Machine learning is humanity's mathematical attempt to map the way God designed us to learn; we are in a perpetual pursuit of understanding the magnificent architecture of our own creation. Throughout this essay, I use the terms "God" and "Allah SWT" interchangeably to refer to the Creator. When I refer to "we" or "our," I am speaking of our collective human experience as we navigate ambition, learning, and the pursuit of excellence.

The Mechanism: Learning Through the Game

To understand this architectural map, we can look at the analogy of achieving a major goal, such as winning a basketball championship. This process begins with the initialization phase, where we identify exactly what we are trying to achieve. We break down the mechanics required for mastery: free throws, layups, pacing, and defensive positioning. In machine learning, these are the variables that define our model. They are the specific factors that dictate whether you raise a trophy or pack up early. Once identified, we must weigh them, deciding how much time to spend shooting versus studying footage.

This leads to forward-propagation. You don't just sit on the bench waiting for a sudden revelation or divine insight to make you a pro; you have to plug in values for those variables. You test a training regimen that makes sense in your head. You grind through the season, but perhaps you don't achieve the stats you desired. This gap between the reality you achieved and the result you intended is what we call the "loss."

During the postseason, you dissect that failure. This is when backpropagation kicks in. We trace our steps backward, isolating which variables went wrong. Greatness, however, rarely comes from one adjustment. That is where gradient descent takes over: the relentless, iterative repetition of backpropagation. You adjust, you fail, you learn, and you step closer to the result you wanted until your skill becomes undeniable.

The Pursuit: Ambition and the Uncaused Cause

It's tempting to view life as a simple gradient descent model, but reality is far more complex. Life has an infinite number of variables, and we truly control almost none of them. If we try to trace every cause back to a previous one, such as why we fell sick or why a specific opportunity vanished, we fall into an infinite regress, a never-ending chain of "why." Logically, this chain must eventually be pushed by something. If everything in our world relies on something else to exist, there must be an "Uncaused Cause" that is eternally reliable. That Cause is God.

If we control so little, how do we justify ambition? Islam provides the beautiful parable of the birds to answer this. We're promised that if we rely on God with true trust, we'll be provided for just as they are. But the bird doesn't wait in the nest for a meal to appear. It takes flight early in the morning, entirely unsure of where its provision will come from, yet doing its absolute due diligence to search. When you understand that your provision is guaranteed as long as you put in the work, the scale of your dreams changes. I'm emboldened to attempt pleasing Allah SWT in the grandest way possible because the path, while difficult, was drafted by the Best of Planners.

The Responsibility: The Bow and the Archer

I envision ambition as the drawing of a bowstring. A setback is not a regression; it is the tension before the release. You feel like you're being pulled backward, the pressure is agonizing, and you are close to your breaking point. But the most powerful shots are delivered from that exact position of tension. However, while we draw our strings back to aim for our goals, we have to look at the archers standing beside us.

What about those whose bowstrings have been violently cut by injustice? In Palestine, Syria, Sudan, Kashmir, and among the Uyghurs, there are children in the rubble with an ambition that far outscales ours, yet their tools are broken. Having the choice to struggle toward a passion is a monumental privilege. We live in a world defined by sacrifice; even our parents often broke themselves to give us the opportunities we hold today. Knowing this, we must aim with profound care.

The goal isn't just self-serving greatness. The true goal is to use our voices, our provision, and our choices to help and please Allah SWT in the most impactful way possible. On the Day of Judgment, we'll be asked what we did with our pristine bows. We must silence distractions and hollow leisure. If you can't find the drive in your own ambition, do it for Hind, for Mostafa, for Omar. Do it for the children whose arrows were stolen. Be the archer with frightening accuracy, a resilient bow, and enough arrows and compassion to share with the entire world.

Furthermore, we must recognize that our time is an amanah, a sacred trust granted to us by the Creator. It is not merely a resource to be spent, but a loan for which we are accountable. If you know you are to get the same rizq no matter what, not a sliver more or a sliver less, then you have every reason to use your time to please Allah SWT in the most grand manner possible. You have these blessings for a reason, and you will be asked about how you used them. I pray God gives you the strength to steward that time wisely and the capacity to share your blessings with the world.
